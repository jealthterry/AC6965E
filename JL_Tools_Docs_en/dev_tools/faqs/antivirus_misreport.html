7.8. About Antivirus (False) Alarms on the Tools

Note

This page was missing from the saved files and was restored from an Internet Archive snapshot (2026-08-24); it may differ slightly from the current online version.

Because most of our tools are statically linked, they generally include the Windows CRT (that is, the Windows implementation of LIBC, which contains implementations of functions such as printf and fopen).

This makes antivirus software such as 360 prone to false alarms (possibly because viruses also statically link the CRT, and 360's detection algorithm may be based on the degree of similarity).

In general, if 360 reports a detection of type HEUR/QVM, it is a false alarm (you can also cross-check with other antivirus software, such as Huorong).

Important

If it is another type, your copy of the tool may really be infected; it is best to run a full disk scan to confirm.
